Further Vectors

Q1 Easy 4 marks

Given \(\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}-\mathbf{k}\) and \(\mathbf{b}=3\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}+2\mathbf{k}\), find \(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\).

\(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}=\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i}&\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\\1&2&-1\\3&-1&2\end{vmatrix}=\mathbf{i}(4-1)-\mathbf{j}(2+3)+\mathbf{k}(-1-6)=3\mathbf{i}-5\mathbf{j}-7\mathbf{k}\).

Q2 Easy 5 marks

Find a unit vector perpendicular to both \(\mathbf{u}=2\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k}\) and \(\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{i}+3\mathbf{j}-2\mathbf{k}\).

\(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}=\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i}&\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\\2&-1&1\\1&3&-2\end{vmatrix}=\mathbf{i}(2-3)-\mathbf{j}(-4-1)+\mathbf{k}(6+1)=-\mathbf{i}+5\mathbf{j}+7\mathbf{k}\).

Magnitude \(\sqrt{1+25+49}=\sqrt{75}=5\sqrt{3}\). Unit vector \(\pm\dfrac{1}{5\sqrt{3}}(-\mathbf{i}+5\mathbf{j}+7\mathbf{k})\).

Q3 Easy 4 marks

The points \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{i}\), \(2\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}\) and \(\mathbf{i}+3\mathbf{j}+2\mathbf{k}\) respectively. Find the area of triangle \(ABC\).

\(\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}\), \(\overrightarrow{AC}=3\mathbf{j}+2\mathbf{k}\).

\(\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}=\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i}&\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\\1&1&0\\0&3&2\end{vmatrix}=2\mathbf{i}-2\mathbf{j}+3\mathbf{k}\). Magnitude \(\sqrt{4+4+9}=\sqrt{17}\).

Area \(=\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{17}\).

Q4 Easy 5 marks

Evaluate the scalar triple product \([\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}]=\mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c})\) where \(\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}\), \(\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k}\), \(\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{i}\). Interpret the result geometrically.

\(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}=\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i}&\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\\0&1&1\\1&0&1\end{vmatrix}=\mathbf{i}(1)-\mathbf{j}(-1)+\mathbf{k}(-1)=\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}-\mathbf{k}\).

\(\mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c})=1+1+0=2\). The volume of the parallelepiped formed by the three vectors is \(2\).

Q5 Medium 6 marks

Show that \(\mathbf{a}\times(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c})=(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c})\mathbf{b}-(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})\mathbf{c}\). Verify for \(\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{i}\), \(\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{j}\), \(\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{k}\).

This is the standard vector triple product identity (proof via components or geometric argument).

Verification: left side \(\mathbf{i}\times(\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{k})=\mathbf{i}\times\mathbf{i}=\mathbf{0}\). Right side \((\mathbf{i}\cdot\mathbf{k})\mathbf{j}-(\mathbf{i}\cdot\mathbf{j})\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{0}\). Holds.

Q6 Medium 7 marks

The lines \(L_1:\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}+\lambda(\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}+2\mathbf{k})\) and \(L_2:\mathbf{r}=3\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{k}+\mu(2\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}-\mathbf{k})\) are skew. Find the shortest distance between them.

\(\mathbf{d}_1=(1,-1,2)\), \(\mathbf{d}_2=(2,1,-1)\). \(\mathbf{d}_1\times\mathbf{d}_2=\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i}&\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\\1&-1&2\\2&1&-1\end{vmatrix}=\mathbf{i}(1-2)-\mathbf{j}(-1-4)+\mathbf{k}(1+2)=-\mathbf{i}+5\mathbf{j}+3\mathbf{k}\).

Connecting vector \(\mathbf{a}_2-\mathbf{a}_1=(2,-2,-1)\). Distance \(=\dfrac{|(\mathbf{a}_2-\mathbf{a}_1)\cdot(\mathbf{d}_1\times\mathbf{d}_2)|}{|\mathbf{d}_1\times\mathbf{d}_2|}=\dfrac{|-2-10-3|}{\sqrt{1+25+9}}=\dfrac{15}{\sqrt{35}}=\dfrac{3\sqrt{35}}{7}\).

Q7 Medium 6 marks

Find the volume of the tetrahedron with vertices at \(O\), \(A(1,2,0)\), \(B(0,1,3)\) and \(C(2,0,1)\).

Volume \(=\dfrac{1}{6}|[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}]|=\dfrac{1}{6}\left|\begin{vmatrix}1&2&0\\0&1&3\\2&0&1\end{vmatrix}\right|=\dfrac{1}{6}|1(1-0)-2(0-6)+0|=\dfrac{1}{6}|1+12|=\dfrac{13}{6}\).

Q8 Medium 6 marks

Given that \(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{c}\times\mathbf{d}\) and \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{d}\), show that \((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d})\times(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c})=\mathbf{0}\) does not necessarily hold, but find a condition under which \(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d}\) is parallel to \(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c}\).

From \(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{c}\times\mathbf{d}\) we have \(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c}\times\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{0}\). Expanding \((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d})\times(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c})=\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{d}\times\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{d}\times\mathbf{c}\) requires the given scalar condition and further assumptions to conclude parallelism. In general the cross product need not vanish; a sufficient condition is that the four vectors are coplanar in a suitable sense.

Q9 Hard 8 marks

The plane \(\Pi\) passes through the points \(A(1,0,1)\), \(B(2,1,0)\) and \(C(0,1,2)\). Find a Cartesian equation of \(\Pi\). Find the perpendicular distance from the point \(D(3,3,3)\) to \(\Pi\).

\(\overrightarrow{AB}=(1,1,-1)\), \(\overrightarrow{AC}=(-1,1,1)\). Normal \(\mathbf{n}=\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}=\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i}&\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\\1&1&-1\\-1&1&1\end{vmatrix}=2\mathbf{i}+0\mathbf{j}+2\mathbf{k}=(2,0,2)\).

Plane: \(2(x-1)+2(z-1)=0\Rightarrow x+z=2\).

Distance from \(D\): \(\dfrac{|3+3-2|}{\sqrt{1+0+1}}=\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{2}}=2\sqrt{2}\).

Q10 Hard 7 marks

Prove that \(|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|^2=|\mathbf{a}|^2|\mathbf{b}|^2-(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})^2\). Deduce that \(|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|\le|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\).

Left side: \(|\mathbf{a}|^2|\mathbf{b}|^2\sin^2\theta\). Right side: \(|\mathbf{a}|^2|\mathbf{b}|^2-|\mathbf{a}|^2|\mathbf{b}|^2\cos^2\theta=|\mathbf{a}|^2|\mathbf{b}|^2\sin^2\theta\). Equal.

Since \(\sin^2\theta\le1\), \(|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|\le|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\), with equality when \(\theta=\pm\pi/2\).

Q11 Hard 8 marks

Three vectors \(\mathbf{a}\), \(\mathbf{b}\) and \(\mathbf{c}\) satisfy \(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{0}\). Show that \(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{c}\times\mathbf{a}\). Interpret this result for a triangle.

From \(\mathbf{c}=-(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})\), \(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\). \(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{b}\times(-(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}))=-\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\). Similarly \(\mathbf{c}\times\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\).

For a triangle with sides represented by \(\mathbf{a}\), \(\mathbf{b}\), \(\mathbf{c}\) (closed), the cross products are equal and each has magnitude twice the area of the triangle (and the same direction normal to the plane).

Q12 Hard 7 marks

Find the equation of the plane which contains the line of intersection of the planes \(x+y+z=1\) and \(2x-y+3z=4\), and which is parallel to the vector \(\mathbf{i}-2\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k}\).

The required plane has equation \((x+y+z-1)+\lambda(2x-y+3z-4)=0\), i.e. \((1+2\lambda)x+(1-\lambda)y+(1+3\lambda)z-(1+4\lambda)=0\).

It is parallel to \(\mathbf{d}=(1,-2,1)\), so the normal is perpendicular to \(\mathbf{d}\): \((1+2\lambda)(1)+(1-\lambda)(-2)+(1+3\lambda)(1)=0\).

\(1+2\lambda-2+2\lambda+1+3\lambda=0\Rightarrow 5\lambda=0\Rightarrow\lambda=0\). Plane: \(x+y+z=1\). (In this special case the first plane is already parallel to the given vector; check: \(1-2+1=0\), yes.)
