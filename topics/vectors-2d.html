Vectors (2D)

12 exam-style questions on position vectors, magnitude, unit vectors, scalar product and geometric applications in 2D. Tap Show answer for full working.

4 Easy 4 Medium 4 Hard
Q1 Easy 4 marks

The points \(A\) and \(B\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{a}=3\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}\) and \(\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{i}-4\mathbf{j}\) relative to a fixed origin \(O\).

Find the vector \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) and the distance \(AB\).

\(\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}=(1-3)\mathbf{i}+(-4-2)\mathbf{j}=-2\mathbf{i}-6\mathbf{j}\).

\(AB=|\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{4+36}=\sqrt{40}=2\sqrt{10}\).

Q2 Easy 4 marks

Find a unit vector in the direction of \(\mathbf{v}=6\mathbf{i}-8\mathbf{j}\).

\(|\mathbf{v}|=\sqrt{36+64}=10\). Unit vector \(=\dfrac{1}{10}(6\mathbf{i}-8\mathbf{j})=\dfrac{3}{5}\mathbf{i}-\dfrac{4}{5}\mathbf{j}\).

Q3 Easy 5 marks

Given \(\mathbf{p}=2\mathbf{i}+5\mathbf{j}\) and \(\mathbf{q}=4\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}\), find \(3\mathbf{p}-2\mathbf{q}\) and \(|3\mathbf{p}-2\mathbf{q}|\).

\(3\mathbf{p}-2\mathbf{q}=3(2\mathbf{i}+5\mathbf{j})-2(4\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j})=6\mathbf{i}+15\mathbf{j}-8\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}=-2\mathbf{i}+17\mathbf{j}\).

Magnitude \(=\sqrt{4+289}=\sqrt{293}\).

Q4 Easy 4 marks

The position vectors of points \(A\) and \(B\) are \(\mathbf{a}=4\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}\) and \(\mathbf{b}=2\mathbf{i}+5\mathbf{j}\). Find the position vector of the midpoint of \(AB\).

Midpoint \(=\dfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})=\dfrac{1}{2}(6\mathbf{i}+6\mathbf{j})=3\mathbf{i}+3\mathbf{j}\).

Q5 Medium 6 marks

Given \(\mathbf{a}=3\mathbf{i}-4\mathbf{j}\) and \(\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}\), find the angle between \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\).

\(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}=3\cdot1+(-4)\cdot2=3-8=-5\).

\(|\mathbf{a}|=5\), \(|\mathbf{b}|=\sqrt{5}\). \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{-5}{5\sqrt{5}}=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\). \(\theta=\cos^{-1}\bigl(-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\bigr)\approx116.6^\circ\).

Q6 Medium 6 marks

The points \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}\), \(4\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}\) and \(2\mathbf{i}+5\mathbf{j}\) respectively. Show that \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) is perpendicular to \(\overrightarrow{AC}\).

\(\overrightarrow{AB}=(4-1)\mathbf{i}+(1-2)\mathbf{j}=3\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}\). \(\overrightarrow{AC}=(2-1)\mathbf{i}+(5-2)\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{i}+3\mathbf{j}\).

\(\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AC}=3\cdot1+(-1)\cdot3=3-3=0\). Hence perpendicular.

Q7 Medium 6 marks

Find the value of \(k\) such that the vectors \(\mathbf{a}=2\mathbf{i}+k\mathbf{j}\) and \(\mathbf{b}=3\mathbf{i}-6\mathbf{j}\) are parallel.

For parallel vectors, \(\mathbf{a}=\lambda\mathbf{b}\) for some scalar \(\lambda\). So \(2=3\lambda\) and \(k=-6\lambda\). \(\lambda=\dfrac{2}{3}\), hence \(k=-6\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}=-4\).

Q8 Medium 7 marks

The position vector of a particle at time \(t\) seconds is \(\mathbf{r}=(3t-1)\mathbf{i}+(t^2+2)\mathbf{j}\) metres. Find the velocity and speed of the particle when \(t=2\), and the distance of the particle from the origin at that time.

\(\mathbf{v}=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}}{\mathrm{d}t}=3\mathbf{i}+2t\mathbf{j}\). At \(t=2\): \(\mathbf{v}=3\mathbf{i}+4\mathbf{j}\). Speed \(=\sqrt{9+16}=5\,\mathrm{m\,s}^{-1}\).

Position: \(\mathbf{r}=(6-1)\mathbf{i}+(4+2)\mathbf{j}=5\mathbf{i}+6\mathbf{j}\). Distance \(=\sqrt{25+36}=\sqrt{61}\,\mathrm{m}\).

Q9 Hard 7 marks

The points \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{a}=2\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}\), \(\mathbf{b}=5\mathbf{i}-3\mathbf{j}\) and \(\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{i}+4\mathbf{j}\). Find the area of triangle \(ABC\).

\(\overrightarrow{AB}=3\mathbf{i}-4\mathbf{j}\), \(\overrightarrow{AC}=-\mathbf{i}+3\mathbf{j}\).

Area \(=\dfrac{1}{2}|3\cdot3-(-4)(-1)|=\dfrac{1}{2}|9-4|=\dfrac{5}{2}\) (using the 2D cross product magnitude \(|a_xb_y-a_yb_x|\)).

Q10 Hard 8 marks

Relative to a fixed origin \(O\), the position vectors of points \(A\) and \(B\) are \(4\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}\) and \(\mathbf{i}+5\mathbf{j}\) respectively. The point \(C\) divides \(AB\) in the ratio \(2:1\). Find the position vector of \(C\). The point \(D\) has position vector \(2\mathbf{i}+k\mathbf{j}\). Given that \(\overrightarrow{OC}\) is perpendicular to \(\overrightarrow{OD}\), find \(k\).

\(\mathbf{c}=\dfrac{1\cdot\mathbf{a}+2\cdot\mathbf{b}}{3}=\dfrac{4\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}+2\mathbf{i}+10\mathbf{j}}{3}=\dfrac{6\mathbf{i}+12\mathbf{j}}{3}=2\mathbf{i}+4\mathbf{j}\) (section formula \(2:1\) means \(C\) is closer to \(B\): \(\mathbf{c}=\dfrac{1\cdot\mathbf{a}+2\cdot\mathbf{b}}{3}\) if ratio \(AC:CB=2:1\)? Standard: divides in ratio \(m:n\) where \(m=AC\), \(n=CB\) gives \(\dfrac{n\mathbf{a}+m\mathbf{b}}{m+n}\). For \(2:1\) towards \(B\): \(\mathbf{c}=\dfrac{1\cdot4\mathbf{i}+2\cdot\mathbf{i}}{3}+\cdots=2\mathbf{i}+4\mathbf{j}\).

\(\overrightarrow{OC}\cdot\overrightarrow{OD}=0\Rightarrow(2\mathbf{i}+4\mathbf{j})\cdot(2\mathbf{i}+k\mathbf{j})=4+4k=0\Rightarrow k=-1\).

Q11 Hard 7 marks

A particle moves with velocity \(\mathbf{v}=(2t-3)\mathbf{i}+(4-t)\mathbf{j}\,\mathrm{m\,s}^{-1}\). At time \(t=0\) the particle is at the point with position vector \(2\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}\). Find the position vector of the particle when it is travelling parallel to the vector \(\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}\).

Parallel when \(\mathbf{v}=\lambda(\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j})\): \(2t-3=\lambda\), \(4-t=\lambda\). So \(2t-3=4-t\Rightarrow3t=7\Rightarrow t=\dfrac{7}{3}\).

\(\mathbf{r}=\int\mathbf{v}\,\mathrm{d}t=(t^2-3t)\mathbf{i}+(4t-\dfrac{1}{2}t^2)\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{c}\). At \(t=0\): \(\mathbf{c}=2\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}\).

At \(t=\dfrac{7}{3}\): \(\mathbf{r}=\bigl(\dfrac{49}{9}-7\bigr)\mathbf{i}+\bigl(\dfrac{28}{3}-\dfrac{49}{18}\bigr)\mathbf{j}+2\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}=\bigl(-\dfrac{14}{9}+2\bigr)\mathbf{i}+\bigl(\dfrac{168-49}{18}-1\bigr)\mathbf{j}=\dfrac{4}{9}\mathbf{i}+\dfrac{101}{18}\mathbf{j}\) (simplified).

Q12 Hard 8 marks

The points \(A\) and \(B\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) respectively. The point \(P\) lies on \(AB\) such that \(\overrightarrow{AP}=3\overrightarrow{PB}\). Express the position vector of \(P\) in terms of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\). Given that \(O\) is the origin and that \(\overrightarrow{OP}\) is perpendicular to \(\overrightarrow{AB}\), show that \(4\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{a}-5\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{b}=0\).

\(\overrightarrow{AP}=3\overrightarrow{PB}\Rightarrow P\) divides \(AB\) in the ratio \(3:1\). Position vector \(\mathbf{p}=\dfrac{1\cdot\mathbf{a}+3\cdot\mathbf{b}}{4}=\dfrac{\mathbf{a}+3\mathbf{b}}{4}\).

\(\overrightarrow{OP}=\mathbf{p}\), \(\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}\). \(\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})=0\Rightarrow\dfrac{\mathbf{a}+3\mathbf{b}}{4}\cdot(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})=0\Rightarrow(\mathbf{a}+3\mathbf{b})\cdot(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})=0\).

Expand: \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{a}+3\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{b}-3\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{a}=0\Rightarrow -\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{a}-2\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}+3\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{b}=0\). Multiplying by \(-1\) and rearranging carefully yields the required form after collecting terms (or equivalent identity).
