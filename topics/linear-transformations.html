Linear Transformations

12 exam-style questions on 2D/3D transformations, invariant lines/points and successive maps. Tap Show answer for full working.

4 Easy 4 Medium 4 Hard
Q1Easy3 marks

Write down the \( 2\times 2 \) matrix that represents a reflection in the line \( y = x \).

\( \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \)

Q2Easy3 marks

Find the matrix that represents an anticlockwise rotation through \( 90^\circ \) about the origin.

\( \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \)

Q3Easy4 marks

The matrix \( \mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix} \) represents a transformation. Describe the geometric effect of the transformation fully.

Stretch scale factor 2 parallel to the \( x \)-axis and stretch scale factor 3 parallel to the \( y \)-axis (or enlargement with different scale factors along the axes).

Q4Easy4 marks

Find the image of the point \( (3, -1) \) under the transformation represented by \( \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \).

\( \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \). Image is \( (1, -1) \).

Q5Medium5 marks

The transformation \( T \) is represented by \( \mathbf{M} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \). Describe \( T \) geometrically and find the image of the unit square with vertices \( (0,0) \), \( (1,0) \), \( (1,1) \), \( (0,1) \).

\( T \) is a clockwise rotation through \( 90^\circ \) about the origin (or anticlockwise \( 270^\circ \)).

Images: \( (0,0)\to(0,0) \), \( (1,0)\to(0,-1) \), \( (1,1)\to(1,-1) \), \( (0,1)\to(1,0) \).

Q6Medium6 marks

Find the \( 2\times 2 \) matrix that represents a reflection in the line \( y = -x \), followed by an enlargement scale factor 2 centred at the origin.

Reflection in \( y=-x \): \( \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \). Enlargement SF 2: \( \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \).

Combined (reflection first): \( \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -2 \\ -2 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \).

Q7Medium6 marks

The matrix \( \mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} \) represents a linear transformation. Find the invariant lines through the origin.

Invariant lines satisfy \( \mathbf{A}\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix} = \lambda \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix} \). Eigenvalues: \( \det(A-\lambda I)=(3-\lambda)^2-1=0 \) ⇒ \( \lambda=4 \) or \( \lambda=2 \).

For \( \lambda=4 \): \( (A-4I)\mathbf{v}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{v}=0 \) ⇒ \( y=x \). Line \( y=x \).

For \( \lambda=2 \): \( (A-2I)=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix} \) ⇒ \( y=-x \). Line \( y=-x \).

Q8Medium5 marks

Show that the transformation represented by \( \mathbf{B} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \) maps the whole plane onto a single line, and find the equation of that line.

\( \det B = 4-4=0 \), singular. Image of \( (x,y) \) is \( (x+2y, 2x+4y) = (x+2y)(1,2) \). All image points lie on the line \( Y=2X \).

Q9Hard7 marks

The transformation \( T \) is represented by \( \mathbf{M} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \). Find all invariant points and all invariant lines of \( T \).

Invariant points: \( \mathbf{M}\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r} \) ⇒ \( (M-I)\mathbf{r}=0 \) ⇒ \( \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}=0 \) ⇒ \( y=0 \) and \( x+y=0 \) ⇒ only the origin.

Invariant lines through origin: eigenvalues of \( M \) are both 2 (repeated). Eigenspace: \( (M-2I)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix} \) ⇒ \( y=0 \). Only the \( x \)-axis is an invariant line through the origin.

(Non-origin lines of the form \( y=c \) are mapped to \( y=2c \), so only \( c=0 \).)

Q10Hard7 marks

Find the matrix that represents a rotation through \( \theta \) anticlockwise about the origin, followed by a reflection in the \( x \)-axis. Hence find the single matrix for \( \theta = \pi/3 \).

Rotation: \( R = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \). Reflection in \( x \)-axis: \( F = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \).

Combined (rotation first): \( F R = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & -\cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \).

For \( \theta=\pi/3 \): \( \begin{pmatrix} 1/2 & -\sqrt{3}/2 \\ -\sqrt{3}/2 & -1/2 \end{pmatrix} \).

Q11Hard8 marks

The linear transformation \( T \) in the plane is given by \( \mathbf{r}' = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{r} \) where \( \mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 6 \end{pmatrix} \). Find the equation of the image of the line \( x + 2y = 1 \) under \( T \).

\( \det A=0 \), so image of the plane is the line through the origin in direction \( (1,3) \) (column space). Points on \( x+2y=1 \) map to \( \begin{pmatrix}x+2y\\3x+6y\end{pmatrix} = (x+2y)\begin{pmatrix}1\\3\end{pmatrix} = 1\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1\\3\end{pmatrix} \). Every point on the original line maps to the single point \( (1,3) \). The image is the single point \( (1,3) \) (degenerate).

Q12Hard8 marks

In 3D, the matrix \( \mathbf{P} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \) represents a transformation. Describe the geometric effect of \( P \) and find the image of the plane \( z=0 \).

\( P \) fixes the \( x \)-axis and rotates the \( yz \)-plane by \( 90^\circ \) ( \( (y,z)\mapsto(-z,y) \) ), i.e. a rotation of \( 90^\circ \) about the \( x \)-axis (from \( y \) toward \( z \) or check orientation).

Plane \( z=0 \): points \( (x,y,0) \) map to \( (x, 0, y) \). Image is the plane \( y=0 \) (the \( xz \)-plane).
