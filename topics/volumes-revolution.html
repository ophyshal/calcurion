Volumes of Revolution

12 exam-style questions on volumes about the \(x\)- and \(y\)-axes, parametric curves and modelling. Tap Show answer for full working.

4 Easy 4 Medium 4 Hard
Q1Easy4 marks

The region bounded by the curve \( y = x^2 \), the \(x\)-axis and the lines \( x = 1 \) and \( x = 2 \) is rotated through \( 2\pi \) radians about the \(x\)-axis. Find the exact volume of the solid generated.

\( V = \pi \int_1^2 (x^2)^2 \, dx = \pi \int_1^2 x^4 \, dx = \pi \left[ \dfrac{x^5}{5} \right]_1^2 = \pi \left( \dfrac{32}{5} - \dfrac{1}{5} \right) = \dfrac{31\pi}{5} \)

Q2Easy4 marks

The region bounded by \( y = \sqrt{x} \), the \(x\)-axis and \( x = 4 \) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. Find the volume of the solid of revolution.

\( V = \pi \int_0^4 (\sqrt{x})^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^4 x \, dx = \pi \left[ \dfrac{x^2}{2} \right]_0^4 = 8\pi \)

Q3Easy5 marks

Find the volume generated when the region bounded by \( y = e^x \), the \(x\)-axis, \( x = 0 \) and \( x = 1 \) is rotated through \( 2\pi \) about the \(x\)-axis.

\( V = \pi \int_0^1 e^{2x} \, dx = \pi \left[ \dfrac{1}{2} e^{2x} \right]_0^1 = \dfrac{\pi}{2}(e^2 - 1) \)

Q4Easy4 marks

The region bounded by \( y = 2x \), the \(y\)-axis and \( y = 4 \) is rotated about the \(y\)-axis. Find the volume of the solid generated.

\( x = y/2 \). \( V = \pi \int_0^4 (y/2)^2 \, dy = \dfrac{\pi}{4} \left[ y^3/3 \right]_0^4 = \dfrac{16\pi}{3} \)

Q5Medium6 marks

The region \( R \) is bounded by \( y = \sin x \), the \(x\)-axis and the lines \( x = 0 \) and \( x = \pi \). Find the volume of the solid formed when \( R \) is rotated through \( 2\pi \) about the \(x\)-axis.

\( V = \pi \int_0^\pi \sin^2 x \, dx \). Use \( \sin^2 x = (1-\cos 2x)/2 \):

\( V = \dfrac{\pi}{2} \left[ x - \frac12\sin 2x \right]_0^\pi = \dfrac{\pi^2}{2} \)

Q6Medium6 marks

The region bounded by \( y = 1/x \), the lines \( x = 1 \), \( x = 2 \) and the \(x\)-axis is rotated about the \(y\)-axis. Find the volume using cylindrical shells.

\( V = 2\pi \int_1^2 x \cdot (1/x) \, dx = 2\pi \int_1^2 1 \, dx = 2\pi \)

Q7Medium6 marks

The curve \( y = 4 - x^2 \) meets the \(x\)-axis at \( A \) and \( B \). The region bounded by the curve and the \(x\)-axis is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. Find the volume of the solid generated.

Roots \( x = \pm 2 \). \( V = \pi \int_{-2}^{2} (4-x^2)^2 \, dx = 2\pi \int_0^2 (16 - 8x^2 + x^4)\, dx = 2\pi \big[16x - 8x^3/3 + x^5/5\big]_0^2 = 2\pi(32 - 64/3 + 32/5) = 512\pi/15 \)

Q8Medium5 marks

Find the volume generated when the region bounded by \( y = \ln x \), the \(x\)-axis and \( x = e \) is rotated about the \(y\)-axis.

\( x = e^y \), limits \( y=0 \) to \( y=1 \). \( V = \pi \int_0^1 e^{2y}\, dy = \dfrac{\pi}{2}(e^2 - 1) \)

Q9Hard7 marks

The region \( R \) is bounded by the curves \( y = x^2 \) and \( y = 2x \). Find the volume of the solid formed when \( R \) is rotated through \( 2\pi \) about the \(x\)-axis.

Intersect at \( x=0,2 \). Washer: \( V = \pi \int_0^2 \big((2x)^2 - (x^2)^2\big)\, dx = \pi \int_0^2 (4x^2 - x^4)\, dx = \pi\big[4x^3/3 - x^5/5\big]_0^2 = 64\pi/15 \)

Q10Hard7 marks

The finite region bounded by \( y = \sqrt{x} \), \( y = 0 \) and \( x = 4 \) is rotated about the line \( x = 4 \). Find the volume of the solid generated.

Shells: \( V = 2\pi \int_0^4 (4-x)\sqrt{x}\, dx = 2\pi \int_0^4 (4x^{1/2} - x^{3/2})\, dx = 2\pi \big[ \frac83 x^{3/2} - \frac25 x^{5/2} \big]_0^4 = 256\pi/15 \)

Q11Hard8 marks

The region bounded by \( y = x \) and \( y = x^2 \) is rotated about the \(y\)-axis. Find the exact volume of the solid of revolution.

Intersect \( x=0,1 \). About \( y \)-axis: \( V = \pi \int_0^1 \big((\sqrt{y})^2 - y^2\big)\, dy = \pi \int_0^1 (y - y^2)\, dy = \pi/6 \)

Q12Hard8 marks

A solid is formed by rotating the region bounded by \( y = \cos x \), \( 0 \leq x \leq \pi/2 \), about the \(y\)-axis. Show that the volume is \( \pi(\pi - 2) \).

Shells: \( V = 2\pi \int_0^{\pi/2} x\cos x\, dx \). Parts \( u=x \), \( dv=\cos x\,dx \): \( 2\pi\big[x\sin x\big]_0^{\pi/2} - 2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x\, dx = \pi^2 - 2\pi\big[-\cos x\big]_0^{\pi/2} = \pi^2 - 2\pi = \pi(\pi-2) \).
